Standing orders revised day 61

  • Colder than −50 °C, or a blizzard warning standing: nobody goes out. Not for five minutes, not for a photograph.
  • Sign out. Say where. Say when you are back. Take the radio and turn it on.
  • Vents and exhaust intakes are checked every watch. A buried exhaust does not announce itself — it just fills the building.
  • Comms window to the mainland is 21:00–21:40. Outside that we are on our own, which is the normal condition.
  • The generator is the whole building. Anything odd about the sound of it wakes the mech, day or night.
  • An amended return time is signed and timed. A board nobody can be held to is worse than no board.
  • Everything that happens on a watch goes in the one book, in the order it happened. A sign-out, a reading off the masts and a dip of the day tank are not three different records kept in three different places. They are three lines, and the only thing they have in common is that somebody wrote each one down at a time.
  • Three trips out in a day and the duty officer sends somebody else. The tally is kept per person, not for the station — a crew of twelve averaging one trip each is an ordinary day, and one person doing all twelve is how people get hurt.

Haldane Station is not a real place. It is the demonstration world for a college .NET course, and everything above is invented — the crew, the log, the weather. The station is named for the Haldanes, physiologists of dangerous air, which is why the thing most likely to kill you here is a blocked exhaust rather than the cold.
