ABOUT THIS BOARD

the part that isn't in character
================================================================================================================================================================

Haldane Station is not real

It is an invented Antarctic research station, and it exists to be the setting for demonstrations in a college programming course. The crew are invented. The log is invented. So are the weather, the generator, and the twelve people.

Nobody is out on the ice. If you came here worried about that, you can stop.

Why a terminal

In the story: the station is five hundred kilometres from anybody. There is no website out there, no phone, no app, and no second screen. A console in the corridor is genuinely the whole interface to the building, so the plainness is the setting rather than a limitation.

Out of the story: plenty of real software has no web page in front of it. It runs in a terminal, keeps track of things, and somebody depends on it being right. Haldane is a place where that kind of program is the obvious thing to build.

Where the name comes from

The Haldanes were a family of British physiologists who, between them, spent their careers studying air that kills you.

John Scott Haldane worked on respiration and gas poisoning, produced the first decompression tables, and is the reason canaries went down coal mines — he established them as living detectors for carbon monoxide. His son J.B.S. Haldane continued the family tradition largely by experimenting on himself in pressure chambers.

Which is why the most dangerous thing at this station is not the cold. It is a blocked exhaust.

The clock, and why the numbers look odd

Haldane keeps UTC+5, which is what the real stations at that longitude do. Every time on the board is 24-hour — 14:20 is twenty past two in the afternoon, and the comms window at 21:00 is nine in the evening. Nobody at a research station writes 9 PM; too much depends on not misreading it.

Right now the station is … — which is roughly the point. It is a long way from anywhere, including from you.

Why the board keeps changing

It gets a little fuller as the term goes on. Panels appear; there is more written down than there was.

There is no plot. Nothing happens here that you have to keep up with, no episode to miss, and nothing from one week that you need to remember to understand the next. It is the same station, further along — the way a real duty board fills up over a winter.

Do you need to read any of this?

No. Nothing on this site is graded, nothing is submitted from it, and no assignment will ever ask you to look at it. It is not homework and it is not a hint.

It is here because a station with a name and a generator and twelve people in it is more interesting to write programs about than a blank screen — and because some people like knowing where a thing came from.
